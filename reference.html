Reference and Download

What the numbers mean, how they were measured, what they do and do not support — and the files themselves. Everything on this page applies to every other tab.

What is on this site

  • Browse the atlas — look up any protein and position: the reference residue and codon, where a single substitution's supply goes, and the rate of every reachable amino-acid change.
  • MRA Table — the curated resistance and antibody-escape tables for all five drug classes and three bNAb epitope classes, each with rates, evidence and citations; and a custom query for your own list of mutations.
  • Your sequence — submit up to 10 sequences. Each one you check is drawn on one genome track; the pie and the flow chart use the sequence you select. Amino-acid changes and codon paths for every submitted sequence are merged into one table each, with a sample column, and can be downloaded.

What the rates mean

Nucleotide rate — for a position and one of 12 substitutions (or an insertion or deletion), the per-replication rate.

Amino-acid mutability (mu_aa) — for a codon and a target amino acid, the rate summed over every nucleotide path to that amino acid. One codon often reaches an amino acid by more than one route, so the paths are summed. The path count and the fewest substitutions needed are reported with it.

Supply is not frequency. Every rate here is mutational supply — how often a change is generated per round of replication. How often a variant is actually seen in patients is supply filtered through selection, treatment history, founder effects and sampling. The tables and charts on this site report supply. They do not report how often a variant is seen in patients.

Coverage and coordinates

Results use HXB2 protein numbering (RT 103, PR 46, IN 148), the clinical and Stanford HIVDB standard. Sequence results also carry your own coordinates; the NL4-3 reference position is kept as a traceability column.

Whole-genome HXB2 nucleotide coordinates are not reported: the reference starts at the gag start codon with indels downstream, so no single offset holds, and the shipped HXB2 map is amino-acid level.

How it was measured

Rates come from ERR-Seq: UMI-based consensus sequencing that reads the mutations from a single round of replication on an unmodified viral genome. Wild-type and control libraries are fit together, so the published rate is control-corrected — the biological rate per site per replication, with sequencing and PCR error removed.

Reading the curated tables

Every table on the MRA Table page carries the same five columns. Mutation is the amino-acid change, in standard protein numbering for the drug classes and HXB2 numbering for antibody escape. Amino Acid Mutability is mu_aa: the rate of reaching that amino acid from the NL4-3 codon, summed over every nucleotide path, per site per replication. Minimum Nucleotides is the fewest substitutions the change requires, and Paths the number of distinct nucleotide routes to it; 1-, 2- and 3-nucleotide changes are all listed.

The rate is a property of the NL4-3 codon, not of the amino-acid change in the abstract: a virus spelling the same wild-type residue with a different synonymous codon can have a substantially different rate for the same substitution, sometimes by more than an order of magnitude, usually because one codon needs an extra nucleotide. Rates here are from the NL4-3 codon throughout.

Two things the tables deliberately do not show. Indel-coded drug-resistance mutations (Stanford's ~ and # codes) are excluded: they are real resistance mechanisms but not amino-acid substitutions, so the model estimates no mutability for them; each table reports how many were left out. Antibody-escape changes whose published wild-type residue NL4-3 does not carry are likewise counted rather than listed, because the atlas has no rate for them. Per-row antibody, evidence strength and citation for every escape change are in bnab_catalog.csv and docs/BNAB_PROVENANCE.md.

The model bundle

The fitted model as one JSON file: every type coefficient and context delta, all 13 G→A intercepts and all six position splines, as coefficients and pre-evaluated per position. The sequence tool runs on this, and it reproduces every rate in the nucleotide table.
