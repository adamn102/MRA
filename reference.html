Reference and Download

What the numbers mean, how they were measured, what they do and do not support — and the files themselves. Everything on this page applies to every other tab.

What is on this site

  • Browse the atlas — look up any protein and position: the reference residue and codon, where a single substitution's supply goes, and the rate of every reachable amino-acid change.
  • MRA Table — the curated resistance and antibody-escape tables for all five drug classes and three bNAb epitope classes, each with rates, evidence and citations; and a custom query for your own list of mutations.
  • Your sequence — submit one sequence or a FASTA batch. Each gets its own atlas from your codons and context, a supply track along the genome, and a comparison to NL4-3.
  • Batch analysis — submit up to 250 sequences at once and compare them codon by codon: what residues and codons the set carries at each position, how mutable each one is, and where its supply goes.

What the rates mean

Nucleotide rate — for a position and one of 12 substitutions (or an insertion or deletion), the per-replication rate.

Amino-acid mutability (mu_aa) — for a codon and a target amino acid, the rate summed over every nucleotide path to that amino acid. One codon often reaches an amino acid by more than one route, so the paths are summed. The path count and the fewest substitutions needed are reported with it.

Supply is not frequency. Every rate here is mutational supply — how often a change is generated per round of replication. How often a variant is actually seen in patients is supply filtered through selection, treatment history, founder effects and sampling. Where both are available they are shown side by side and never conflated.

Coverage and coordinates

Results use HXB2 protein numbering (RT 103, PR 46, IN 148), the clinical and Stanford HIVDB standard. Sequence results also carry your own coordinates; the NL4-3 reference position is kept as a traceability column.

Whole-genome HXB2 nucleotide coordinates are not reported: the reference starts at the gag start codon with indels downstream, so no single offset holds, and the shipped HXB2 map is amino-acid level.

How it was measured

Rates come from ERR-Seq: UMI-based consensus sequencing that reads the mutations from a single round of replication on an unmodified viral genome. Wild-type and control libraries are fit together, so the published rate is control-corrected — the biological rate per site per replication, with sequencing and PCR error removed.

Reading the curated tables

Each row carries its own codon decomposition. Wild-type codon options lists every synonymous codon the wild-type residue could be written as and the rate this change would have from each; * marks the codon NL4-3 carries, and (2nt) marks a codon that needs more than one substitution to reach the target. Codon sensitivity is the fold range across those options — how much the rate would move if the virus spelled the wild-type residue differently. A dash means the residue has a single codon, so codon usage cannot move it at all. Where the range is large it is usually because one codon needs an extra nucleotide, which the codon list makes visible.

Two further distinctions the tables keep separate: codon optimization (the rate varies among synonymous codons at equal accessibility) versus a change in the number of nucleotide steps (categorical, and usually the larger effect); and a specific escape change versus glycan sequon loss, which is summed over three codons and labelled as such.

The model bundle

The fitted model as one JSON file: every type coefficient and context delta, all 13 G→A intercepts and all six position splines, as coefficients and pre-evaluated per position. The sequence tool runs on this, and it reproduces every rate in the nucleotide table.
