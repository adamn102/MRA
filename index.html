HIV-1 Mutation Rate Atlas

How often every mutation arises across the HIV-1 genome, per round of replication. Rates vary more than 1,000-fold from one site to the next. This is mutational supply — what the virus generates, before selection acts on it.

What the rates mean

Nucleotide rate — for a position and one of 12 substitutions (or an insertion or deletion), the per-replication rate.

Amino-acid mutability (mu_aa) — for a codon and a target amino acid, the rate summed over every nucleotide path to that amino acid. One codon often reaches an amino acid by more than one route, so the paths are summed. The path count and the fewest substitutions needed are reported with it.

Coverage and coordinates

Results use HXB2 protein numbering (RT 103, PR 46, IN 148), the clinical and Stanford HIVDB standard. Sequence results also carry your own coordinates; the NL4-3 reference position is kept as a traceability column.

Whole-genome HXB2 nucleotide coordinates are not reported: the reference starts at the gag start codon with indels downstream, so no single offset holds, and the shipped HXB2 map is amino-acid level.

How it was measured

Rates come from ERR-Seq: UMI-based consensus sequencing that reads the mutations from a single round of replication on an unmodified viral genome. Wild-type and control libraries are fit together, so the published rate is control-corrected — the biological rate per site per replication, with sequencing and PCR error removed.
