HIV-1 Mutation Rate Atlas

A position-, mutation-type- and context-resolved map of de novo mutation rates across the HIV-1 genome, measured by ERR-Seq — UMI-based consensus sequencing of mutations accumulating in a single round of replication on an unmodified viral genome. Counts from wild-type libraries are modelled jointly with a control library capturing sequencing and PCR error, so the released quantity is the control-corrected biological rate per site per replication. Rates vary more than 1,000-fold across the genome.

What you can do here

  • Browse the atlas — query any protein and position for the reference amino acid and codon and the mutability of every reachable amino-acid change at 1, 2 and 3 substitutions, with 95% intervals; or paste a list of mutations such as RT K65R and get them all at once.
  • Your sequence & comparison — submit one sequence or a multi-FASTA batch and get a custom atlas computed from your codons and your local sequence contexts, with the mutational-supply track along the genome, which amino-acid changes have become more or less accessible relative to NL4-3, and where your codons differ — all under each analysed sequence.
  • Download — the underlying tables, a data dictionary and a citation block.

Two quantities

Nucleotide rate. For a reference position and one of 12 base substitutions (plus insertion and deletion), the per-replication rate.

Amino-acid mutability, mu_aa. For a codon and a target amino acid, the rate summed over every nucleotide path from that codon to a codon encoding the target. Summed, not averaged or maximised, because one codon can often reach the same amino acid by more than one route. The number of paths and the minimum number of substitutions required are reported alongside.

Coverage and coordinates

Every result carries HXB2 protein numbering — RT 103, PR 46, IN 148 — because that is the clinical and publication standard and what Stanford HIVDB uses. Results computed from a submitted sequence also carry your own coordinates. The NL4-3 reference position is carried as a third column for traceability; it is an internal coordinate.

Whole-genome HXB2 nucleotide coordinates are not reported. The atlas reference starts at the gag start codon with indels downstream, so a constant offset would be wrong, and the shipped HXB2 mapping is amino-acid level. Protein-level HXB2 numbering is what the data supports.
